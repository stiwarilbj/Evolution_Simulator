# Evolution Simulator
## Website, art direction, and simulation specification

## 1. Product description

See the [visual design specification](evolution-simulator-visual-ui-spec.md) and [component atlas](evolution-simulator-component-atlas.md) for the annotated layouts, color states, and control-by-control design.

Evolution Simulator is an interactive natural selection lab. The first thing you see is a raised ivory square floating in a charcoal room, with olive food pellets scattered across it and little blobs waiting at all four edges. Drag the board to orbit it. Press **Play** to send the blobs foraging. Click one and the camera drops to its height, so you can watch its eyes track food, see a larger blob chase it, and find out whether it gets back to safety. An expandable ribbon beneath the stage turns each day into a small colored flower. Click a flower to watch that day again. Open **Trends** to see whether what happened to that one blob changed the population.

**The 2020 MinuteLabs walkthrough sets the design.** Its opening composition, overlay controls, follow shot, four-petal timeline, Settings sheet, five-plot Trends grid, and filmed experiments are the targets. The [April 22 launch-day source snapshot](https://github.com/minutelabsio/evolution-simulator/tree/3b99f358b70a608f5fb3e34fe8f410ba85aec7f3) is the closest dated public architecture reference. The [later public lab](https://labs.minutelabs.io/evolution-simulator/) fills in the expanded multi-species controls and plots. Primer provides the earlier foraging, inheritance, and food-scarcity experiments. Your confirmed rules take priority where the sources differ. [MinuteLabs walkthrough](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6nMo8T3T0L4), [official launch announcement](https://www.patreon.com/minutelabsio/posts/new-lab-36263009), [Primer’s natural selection simulation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0ZGbIKd0XrM)

| Decision | Default for this website |
| --- | --- |
| Daily clock | One day is one generation. At 1× it takes **10 seconds**. Settings allows **1.0–60.0 seconds** in tenths and changes how much time blobs have. The playback control changes wall-clock speed only. |
| Food, safety, and births | At dawn, fresh seeded food is scattered across the field. Plants and eligible prey each award one credit. Get home alive with one credit to survive. At two credits, turn home and, if safe at dusk, have one offspring. At zero energy, die immediately. |
| Energy | Reset to the species’ full energy every dawn. Plants and prey restore equal amounts by default. Both amounts are editable independently. Active cost scales as **size³ × speed² × sense**. |
| Creatures and inheritance | Each species has its own color and founders. Founding traits are **1–100**. At each birth, independently draw enabled traits from a Gaussian centered on the parent, using the configured true variance. Inherited values can exceed 100 and have a minimum of 1. Body radius grows more slowly past size 100. |
| Camera and model | The raised stage, sculpted blobs, and close-up are 3D. All movement, sensing, predation, and contact use the same 2D plane. |
| Watching a run | **Prepare and watch** prepares a playable first day, then fills in later days in the background. **Watch live** advances the same model as it animates and supports jumping ahead from a partial day with Quick Sim. |
| Measuring a run | The five familiar plots lead Trends; Overview, Births and Deaths, Food, and Age at Death follow. Default trait charts show the mean, 25th and 75th percentiles, min, and max. Switch to **Walkthrough spread** for mean ± one sample standard deviation. Every plot and the ribbon can show the whole community or one species. |

**Visual reference.** The [visual design and UI specification](evolution-simulator-visual-ui-spec.md) sets exact paint, layout, typography, component states, motion, responsive rules, and comparison shots. Use it alongside this plan.

### 2.2 Live, recorded, and focused states

| State | What changes on screen |
| --- | --- |
| **Preparing** | Show the board shell and an explicit **Preparing Day 1** status. Enable the player and Quick Sim when the first track and commit are ready. Show its dawn pose and keep background progress compact in the run line. |
| **Ready or paused** | Show **Play** or **Resume**. In Watch live, the worker waits at the current tick. In prepared playback, the recording waits at its current cursor. Camera, layers, selection, and Settings remain usable. |
| **Playing** | Advance the local day cursor, show the active day and mode, and read the live counts or recorded event counts for the displayed time. |
| **Resolving dusk** | Hold the last pose long enough for counts to update, show births at the home edges, and commit the closing record once. With Play Through on, move to the next available dawn. |
| **Quick Sim** | Hold the displayed pose with a compact progress sheet. Show the computational day, last completed day, last saved day, and target. Trends and already recorded days remain available. |
| **Replay** | Show **Replay · Day N**, an in-day scrubber, and **Back to Live** or **Go to frontier**, depending on the run’s mode. The recorded cursor is independent of the active engine. |
| **Finished or extinct** | Hold the final scene at the requested horizon. After extinction, use an empty board and show the extinction day. Empty days still record zero population through the horizon. Offer Trends, replay, extension, and rerun. |

**Selection and Follow.** Hover a blob with a pointer for a thin red contour. Click or tap it to select it and immediately enter Follow. The camera drops to a low view behind its heading, with the board still visible. **Follow** without an existing selection picks the first living ID in a stable display order. Tapping it again returns smoothly to the saved board view. Tap empty floor to clear the selection. Follow keeps the same ID across dawns when it survives. At death, hold the death pose briefly and keep the last details readable. Show **Follow another** to pick a new living blob, and disable it if none remain. Keyboard users can choose from a searchable **Living blobs** list with identical selection behavior. [MinuteLabs following a blob](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6nMo8T3T0L4&t=103s), [released scene selection and camera](https://github.com/minutelabsio/evolution-simulator/blob/55af956a085189e1f307037555fc4d2b6ee2e95a/src/components/view-screen.vue)

**Current Creature.** Default to the four-petal reference flower at the upper right, about 200 × 200 px on desktop and 120–144 px on a phone. Arrange **age**, **speed**, **size**, and **sense** around an open center, with a dark circular field, a thin ring, and the selected blob’s raw values outside the ring. Use that day’s opening cohort to set the reference range for each petal. Use the current stats scope if it contains the selected creature, otherwise the creature’s species. Keep that range fixed during the shot. Give equal-valued cohorts a neutral mid-length and show a dash for a missing value. Put the **Current Creature** label and help icon below, with scope and scaling in the help. The energy disc lives on the board and the exact energy bar is in Details. [Released Current Creature flower](https://github.com/minutelabsio/evolution-simulator/blob/55af956a085189e1f307037555fc4d2b6ee2e95a/src/components/creature-flower-chart.vue)

**Details and events.** Open the drawer from the creature label or Details button. Show species swatch and glyph, ID, parent, birth day, completed age, exact energy and capacity, credits, raw traits, effective travel rate, body radius, and behavioral reason. Returning blobs show distance and estimated time home. A death adds cause and exact simulated time. Filter its event list by plants, hunts, return, deaths, and births. In replay, selecting an event seeks to that time. In Live, it identifies the actor and offers the recorded jump once that day closes. Keep all information readable through icons with text equivalents and a searchable list.

### 2.3 Layers and camera

| Icon or view | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| **Eye · Sense** | Draw filled pale-cyan discs at each visible creature’s feet using its actual planar range. Overlapping discs darken slightly. **Selected** draws one true circle and **All** draws the cohort. Overhead is useful for comparisons. |
| **Runner · Speed** | Tint bodies from muted dark brown to bright red by inherited speed. Normalize to the raw speed range of the available opening cohorts in the current scope, with the displayed day’s range as a fallback for a fresh Live run. Clamp tints at either end and use a mid-tone for a flat range. Show the range in the legend and preserve species identity with a small marker and the selected label. Compare uses a single range across both runs. |
| **Battery · Energy** | Draw a small flat disc near the creature’s feet. Its color shifts from gold at full energy to gray at zero according to the actual remaining fraction. Update it at each simulated change. The Details bar shows the value and capacity. |
| **Utensils · Food** | Show zero, one, or two small olive camera-facing pips at a blob’s feet as it gains credits, plus a check at home. The pips move with the blob and have a text equivalent. |
| **Behavior bubbles** | Use a compact black scalloped bubble with a white icon, initially on the selected creature. Click to collapse it to two small thought dots or expand it. Use the state mapping and transition timing in §3.3. |

Follow starts behind the blob and can be orbited temporarily. Overhead is north-up and orthographic, with optional explicit pan. |

Put a short named tooltip to the left of the selected icon so it stays within the viewport. A small advanced menu supplies Selected/All and behavior, vision, and path toggles. The **Layers** button remembers its toggles when collapsed. On a phone, the same order appears in an accessible anchored menu or bottom sheet if vertical space is tight. [Walkthrough overlays and focused vision](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6nMo8T3T0L4&t=250s), [released layer controls](https://github.com/minutelabsio/evolution-simulator/blob/55af956a085189e1f307037555fc4d2b6ee2e95a/src/components/generation-viewer.vue)

Number fields retain native editing keys. Every icon has an accessible name and a visible focus style. ### 2.4 Current Generation Stats and the Generations dock

The numeric row is **Current Generation Stats** with **Population**, **Average Age**, **Average Age at Death**, **Average Speed**, **Average Size**, and **Average Sense Range** in that order. Include a shared species filter. Live values are labeled **Live · Day N**. While a recording plays, the row reflects the displayed time with **Replay · Day N**. At the end, **Day N · Final** shows the committed values, including births. Historical flowers always use the settled day rows.

The desktop legend is about 184 px wide and has its own collapse control and scale help. In **Walkthrough flower**, the default, show four colored petals in this order: Age gold at the top, then Speed red, Size teal, and Sense cyan clockwise. The Population swatch is cream and belongs to the center disk. Each tile is about 100 px wide with a roughly 92 px circular field, one visible ring, subtle sector dividers, and a small day label below. A petal occupies about 34° of its 90° sector. Give the selected tile a deep teal-green rectangle and a small upward pointer, with an independent high-contrast focus border. In **All six metrics**, add an ivory **Age at Death** petal after Age, shrink each wedge to about 27.5°, and retain the Population center. Age at Death is always present in the numeric row and every tile’s exact readout. [Launch-era ribbon layout](https://github.com/minutelabsio/evolution-simulator/blob/3b99f358b70a608f5fb3e34fe8f410ba85aec7f3/src/components/flower-timeline.vue), [four-petal trait list](https://github.com/minutelabsio/evolution-simulator/blob/3b99f358b70a608f5fb3e34fe8f410ba85aec7f3/src/store/simulation.js), [radial geometry](https://github.com/minutelabsio/evolution-simulator/blob/3b99f358b70a608f5fb3e34fe8f410ba85aec7f3/src/components/flower-chart.vue)

- Use the lowest **individual minimum** and highest **individual maximum** across the committed day rows in the selected scope as the range for each trait. Position each day’s mean within that range, then map it from one-quarter radius to the outer ring. This keeps a genuine low reading visible and lets a single extreme influence the shared scale, as in the source. For Age at Death, use only individual death records. Scale the cream center by the daily population count range, up to a quarter of the tile radius, and use a neutral midpoint for a constant positive series. A zero population gets an empty center. Missing wedges stay empty, with a dash in numeric details. Explain the active range in scale help.
- Click a trait in the legend or a petal on the selected day to rotate that trait to the top across the whole ribbon. Put a small marker on the active legend swatch. The Population center opens its exact count. Click any other tile to select and load its day at 0 seconds. Hover, focus, or long-press any tile for all six raw numbers. Update all tiles together when a new day or scope changes the scale.
- Wheel over the strip or swipe horizontally. Keep spacer space at both ends so the first and last tile can center. Recenter the selected tile in about half a second, or immediately with reduced motion. Virtualize offscreen tiles while preserving keyboard navigation. A jump field, focused day badge, and selected tile all point to the same day.
- Render the current incomplete Live day as an outlined **Live** tile after the committed days. During background preparation show a pending marker with progress. The end-of-strip **More** tile mirrors **More Generations** in the toolbar. If the run went extinct, append a dark circular marker with an original empty-shell icon and the label **Extinct on Day N**. Subsequent committed tiles retain their labels, empty flowers, and zero-population tooltips. Keep the extension control usable. The dock caret collapses the ribbon and legend but leaves the numeric row and time rail.
- Use the radial view by default. Offer a small **Mini line** view for a chosen recent metric. A numeric table is available from either mode. The full dock lives on World and disappears on Trends and About.

### 2.5 Onboarding, About, and the run library

First visit starts with a six-step guided demo. The first screen centers the title, a small reference credit, **Skip** on the left, and a teal **Get started** button on the right. The next screens introduce the tabletop, show click-to-Follow and thought bubbles, turn on the energy layer, contrast a one-credit retreat with a two-credit return, and end on the newborn’s changed traits. Push in from a distant camera view on the first advance. Use brief typewriter captions that can appear instantly with reduced motion. Reveal the World chrome at the end, with a callout to **Settings** and a secondary link to **About**. Use **Back** and **Next** between steps. Finish or skip to the starter Day 1, paused. The library lives in the small run menu and shows each saved experiment’s name, seed, species swatches, rule version, completion state, and saved-through day. **Resume** opens the checkpoint, **View results** opens Trends, **Duplicate setup** opens a draft, and **Branch here** starts after a selected recorded day. Browser Back from a chart replay restores the same run, range, scope, selected day, and scroll position in Trends. Settings uses the same Close behavior via browser Back. ## 3. Visual design and 3D motion

### 3.1 Stage, camera, and visual tokens

Use a white tabletop diamond, gray sidewall, olive pellets, small colored creatures, and an almost black background. At wide zoom the creatures should read as soft raised silhouettes. At Follow distance, their eyes, lobes, and oval shadows show their depth. Apply the reference’s limited palette consistently to navigation, figures, layers, and Settings. [MinuteLabs stage and close-up](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6nMo8T3T0L4&t=75s), [reference style variables](https://github.com/minutelabsio/evolution-simulator/blob/55af956a085189e1f307037555fc4d2b6ee2e95a/src/styles/_variables.scss), [reference accent colors](https://github.com/minutelabsio/evolution-simulator/blob/55af956a085189e1f307037555fc4d2b6ee2e95a/src/config/sougy-colors.js)

| Element | Target |
| --- | --- |
| **Shell and typography** | Charcoal #242424 stage, #171717 dock, #121212 deep overlays, warm white #FFFDFC text, cream #EEEDD6, warm gray for secondary labels. Use Source Sans Pro at 18 px in the shell and 24 px in desktop nav. Use Latin Modern Mono for the player, form values, and uppercase chart headings. Body and control sizes on phones can be smaller with touch targets preserved. |
| **Controls** | Use warm gray icon strokes and pale teal #71D2C0 for active marks and focus. The Settings pill uses muted teal #33786D at rest and #3B8176 on hover. The elapsed rail uses #19AF9A. Tooltips are short, dark with a small caret or teal on the primary layer strip. Keep the dark chart surfaces mostly borderless. |
| **Board** | A 500 × 500 white top at Y = −0.05 with a #AAAAAA, 540 × 540 × 10 slab centered at Y = −6. The 20-unit rim on each side reads as a shallow tabletop edge. Soft visual bevels preserve the square physics. Keep the home strip subdued and visible on demand. |
| **Light and shade** | Matte Lambert-style shading and soft oval contact shadows. Start with ambient fill 0.9 and directional key 0.25 at [100, 200, −10], then tune exposure to separate board and creatures. The launch scene uses #C9D7E6 distance fog from 1,000 to 3,000 scene units. The reference stage has clean faces and little surface texture. |
| **Opening camera** | Perspective, 45° vertical field of view, near plane 1 and far 4,500 scene units. Start near [600, 300, 600] relative to board center, about 45° azimuth and 19.5° elevation. Fit all four edges with dark margin. Begin with orbit damping 0.1, rotate sensitivity 0.2 and zoom sensitivity 0.5; bound elevation and zoom for comfortable framing. |
| **Follow camera** | Ease from the saved board shot to a local chase offset of roughly 100 units behind and 50 above the selected creature, looking about 30 above its position. Derive behind from its smoothed heading and fit the offset for extreme sizes. Use frame-rate-independent damping with the feel of the source’s 5% interpolation per frame at 60 Hz. Settle back into Board in about 1.5 seconds. Avoid clipping through the slab. Keep a little environment in front of the eyes. |
| **Pellets** | Smooth, low olive #83A51E spheres of radius about 2 units, with centers just above the floor and a tiny shadow. Fade in briefly at dawn and disappear from the resource layer at exact contact. |
| **Species and metric colors** | Start with steel Blue #406780. Offer Orange #F47E18 and Pink #CE6591 as optional profiles and support custom distinct colors. Use age #FDDE3B, age at death #FFFDFC, speed #D20F18, size #1CC1AA, sense #07C7FB, and population #EEEDD6 in the ribbon. Thin speed traces use warmer #E97167 for contrast. Use olive births and plants, gray food availability, and contextual crimson deaths with signed position and distinct labels. |

### 3.2 Creature shapes and animation cues

Each blob is a compact pear-like mound with a rounded front, a slightly smaller back lobe, and two tiny black eyes that follow its heading. Blend three rounded lobes into a smooth silhouette, inspired by the released mesh’s blended spheres. Cache this shape in three detail levels and scale it using the body radius curve. The bottom stays close to the floor during squash and lean. Larger blobs look broader and make longer visible strides at the same speed trait. Use a gentle sense of effort in movement and keep the default shot free from noisy trails or constant particle effects. [Walkthrough stride comparison](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6nMo8T3T0L4&t=130s), [released creature rendering](https://github.com/minutelabsio/evolution-simulator/blob/55af956a085189e1f307037555fc4d2b6ee2e95a/src/components/3d-objects/creature.js)

| Moment | Animation and data cue |
| --- | --- |
| **Dawn** | Reveal the pellets on the board and a roughly even spread of creatures on the four edges. Hold the first frame before Play. As motion begins, each blob leans into its own initial direction. |
| **Wander and notice** | Blend turning between snapshots. Use a subtle bob of about 3% of body height. Eyes lead a deliberate turn. A brief target indication belongs to the optional Behavior or Paths layer. |
| **Eat a plant** | Remove the collectible at exact contact. Give the selected close-up a small squash and a short lift/fade of the eaten pellet’s decorative copy. Credit and energy update at the same event. At wide zoom use the clean disappearance. |
| **Hunt and flee** | Lean along travel and show the chase/flee cloud if enabled. Use a small scale/fade cue when the prey is eaten, preserving its identity and the exact capture time in Details and replay. |
| **Return and rest** | The second credit flips the selected creature’s cue to Home. The blob turns and settles on the first legal refuge contact. The credits remain readable while it rests. |

## 5. Planar simulation and generation rules

### 5.1 World coordinates, time, and dawn

- The model owns a 500 × 500 unit square with 2D positions, headings, travel, collision, food placement, vision, and home targets. The renderer places its meshes above those same coordinates. Camera angle, resize, lighting, and body squash affect presentation.
- Run at **20 fixed ticks per simulated second**, 0.05 seconds per tick. Store the duration in integer tenths so 1.0, 10.0, and 60.0 seconds are exactly 20, 200, and 1,200 ticks. A day runs to its set duration in animated mode. When everyone is home or dead, the engine can skip idle work and the display holds the resolved positions until dusk. Quick Sim can commit that idle tail immediately.
- Generate the configured day’s food uniformly inside a 12-unit margin. Each plant has a stable ID. Nearby pellets remain separate resources. Same seed and day use the same ordered positions, so two same-seed food-count experiments can share the prefix of placements.
- Shuffle opening blobs with a dedicated seed stream. Assign edges in rotation after a seeded edge permutation. Counts differ by at most one. On each side, divide the central 90% of the edge into one slot per assigned blob. Jitter each starting point by up to 40% of its slot width in either direction. Clamp the side coordinate away from corners by that blob's radius plus six units. Put its outer body two units inside its assigned edge and aim inward within ±15°. There is a position for every blob even if a crowded lineup overlaps.
- Clear yesterday's plants, statuses, and food credits. Reset every living blob and newborn to its species' daily energy. IDs, species, parent links, birth day, and inherited traits carry forward.

### 5.2 Trait mechanics

Let **S**, **V**, and **R** mean the raw size, speed, and sense traits. Values in charts and inheritance use these raw traits.

| Concept | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| **Size-linked travel rate U** | 100 × (V ÷ 10) × (S ÷ 10) world units per simulated second. At size 10 and speed 10, travel rate is 100 units/s. **Speed only** uses 100 × (V ÷ 10). |
| **Body and contact radius** | Through size 100, radius is 0.25 × S. Above 100, radius is 25 + 25 × (S − 100) ÷ (S − 100 + 100). Growth is continuous and approaches radius 50. At size 200, radius is 37.5. Collision and the body mesh use this curve. Predation and metabolic cost use the raw size trait. |
| **Sensing** | A radius of R logical units from the blob center. The engine checks potential food, prey, and threats inside the circle. The overlay shows that same radius. The search can stop at the board bounds if the trait exceeds the arena's span. |
| **Plant contact** | A swept body circle intersecting the 2-unit plant circle. Touching a pellet can collect it even if the center was outside sensing range. |
| **Hunt eligibility** | Compare raw size. A hunter must be at least 1.20 times its victim's size, the species must be an allowed target, and the hunter must still be feeding-eligible. Size 60 can hunt size 50. Size 59.99 is too small. The distance between the two moving centers must reach the sum of their contact radii. |
| **Turn and wall** | Cap turning at 360 degrees per simulated second. Stop a path at the legal board boundary and reorient next tick. Blobs can pass through other bodies when there is no valid hunt. Home arrival is handled separately. |

MinuteLabs couples speed and size to mimic longer strides and shows speed with a warm tint. Its source separates travel rate from the speed trait. Primer's early speed experiment motivates the independent alternative. [MinuteLabs stride explanation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6nMo8T3T0L4&t=125s), [reference travel and energy methods](https://github.com/minutelabsio/evolution-simulator/blob/55af956a085189e1f307037555fc4d2b6ee2e95a/src/wasm/src/creature/mod.rs)

### 5.3 Movement energy and contact ordering

**Default energy rate per active simulated second** is **100 × (S ÷ 10)³ × (V ÷ 10)² × (R ÷ 20)**. Equivalently, multiply **S³ × V² × R** by a proposed calibration factor of **1 ÷ 20,000**. The baseline blob costs 100 energy/s and starts with 500. That is five seconds of active movement before refills. Doubling size multiplies the rate by eight. Doubling speed multiplies the rate by four. Doubling sense doubles the rate. With size held fixed, twice the speed covers a given distance in half the time and costs twice the energy for that distance.

**Reference additive mode** uses **0.001 × [S³ × (U ÷ 10)² + R]** energy per active second. The launch code adds sight to size cubed times **effective movement speed squared** and charges one ten-thousandth of that per move. The alternative here expresses that at ten reference moves per second using the same unit conversion as travel. Show **Effective speed** explicitly in the equation lesson, since the source couples it to raw speed and size. **Speed only** isolates Primer’s independent speed trait. Settings shows the active formula and current species cost. In either mode, a resting blob at home spends zero movement energy. [Primer’s sensing cost](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0ZGbIKd0XrM&t=330s), [MinuteLabs’ cost explanation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6nMo8T3T0L4&t=197s), [launch-era effective speed and cost](https://github.com/minutelabsio/evolution-simulator/blob/3b99f358b70a608f5fb3e34fe8f410ba85aec7f3/src/wasm/src/creature/mod.rs)

Integrate energy over elapsed active time, including a turn or a moment against the wall. A bite gives exactly one credit and adds the selected energy reward, capped at that species’ starting energy. An active blob at full energy still earns a credit. If it is eaten, the hunter earns one credit for the kill. The prey's existing credits expire. A plant that prey ate earlier still counts in the food record.

Within each tick, compute paths from the same opening poses and check swept contact times. Resolve contact, home arrival, and depletion in chronological order. If events tie, zero energy takes priority, then a valid refuge arrival, then feeding ordered by a seeded stable tie key. Recheck eligibility at contact, so a killed target, eaten pellet, or protected blob cannot be consumed again. A bite strictly before depletion can save the eater. When one event awards the second credit, later contacts in the same tick cannot award extra food to that blob. The next movement intent turns home. High travel speed or large senses change query cost, with bounded movement paths and finite board searches.

### 5.4 Behavior and getting home

**The home zone** is a six-unit-wide refuge strip along the four sides, measured from the outside of each blob's body to the edge. The reachable home point is the nearest point in that strip for the current blob. Ties use a stable edge order. Dawn spawning is active. Refuge protection begins when a fed blob explicitly returns into the strip. A returning blob remains vulnerable until arrival. In the refuge it rests, cannot be eaten, and does not eat or move. [Primer’s return rule](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0ZGbIKd0XrM&t=51s), [MinuteLabs’ return logic](https://github.com/minutelabsio/evolution-simulator/blob/55af956a085189e1f307037555fc4d2b6ee2e95a/src/wasm/src/simulation/behaviours/satisfied.rs)

| State or decision | Next action |
| --- | --- |
| **Zero credits, no threat** | Search for a sensed plant or an eligible prey. Otherwise wander. Every 0.25 simulated seconds, sample a seeded heading change up to 35° either way. When a wandering path would leave the board, steer inward. |
| **One credit, enough reserve** | Keep searching with the same forage priority as a hungry blob. A successful contact makes the second credit and switches its state to Returning. |
| **One credit, reserve reached** | Return with one credit. Estimate time home as remaining distance to the refuge divided by travel rate, plus the smallest turn divided by turn rate, plus a proposed 0.25-second margin. Start returning when either time left or energy divided by active cost is at most that estimate. Keep the chosen home target unless a threat requires a safer reachable edge. |
| **Two credits** | Return immediately along the shortest viable route. Skip forage and chase decisions. |
| **Threat within sense** | Flee the nearest active blob that could legally hunt this blob. If already heading home, prefer the shortest alternate route directed away from the hunter that fits both time and energy remaining. When no better refuge route is reachable, continue toward the selected home point. |
| **Returning with one credit** | Stay on the home route. A plant or eligible prey encountered along the route can supply the second credit. There is no detour. |
| **Home** | Stop immediately and display a rested state and credit count. Stay home until dusk. |
| **Depleted or hunted** | Remove the blob from movement and record its cause and exact time. Its species and age remain available in the record. |

When foraging, pick the nearest sensed plant. For prey, project each eligible sensed blob from its current position and velocity. Estimate the first contact time at the hunter's current travel rate, ignoring candidates outside the hunter's remaining time or energy budget. Prefer the slowest reachable prey, as in the reference. Break ties with earlier contact and then stable ID. Aim at the prey's projected contact point if it would yield food sooner than the nearest plant. Recheck candidates next tick. Actual food awards always come from the swept contacts. The threat and return decisions above take priority over deliberate foraging. [MinuteLabs targeting behavior](https://github.com/minutelabsio/evolution-simulator/blob/55af956a085189e1f307037555fc4d2b6ee2e95a/src/wasm/src/simulation/behaviours/cannibalism.rs)

### 5.5 Dusk, age, and inheritance

Resolve final legal contacts through the last tick. At the day deadline, a living blob with no food dies of **Starvation**. A living fed blob that failed to get home dies of **Missed home**. A rested blob with one credit survives. A rested blob with two survives and gives birth once. Predation and energy depletion have already been recorded. Keep those four death causes mutually exclusive.

A parent keeps its genome throughout its life. For each unlocked trait, a newborn independently samples a Gaussian centered on its parent's raw value with standard deviation √variance. Set any draw below 1 to 1. Keep fractional values and allow values above 100. A locked trait copies its parent's value. The child inherits its parent's species and energy capacity. At dusk the newborn counts in the closing population and its first chance to move or be hunted comes at the next dawn.

**Age** counts completed survived days, as in the walkthrough. Founders start at age 0. Survivors age by one at dusk. Newborns start at age 0. For a death, record how many days the blob had survived at that point, plus a separate exact within-day timestamp. This makes mean age decimal while an individual age remains an integer. The starting rules use age as a measurement. [MinuteLabs age explanation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6nMo8T3T0L4&t=553s)

**Day 1 example.** Ten founders start with ten plants. The field records nine eaten plants and one successful hunt. Four blobs die, one to each cause. The energy-depleted and late-returning blobs had one credit each. Six blobs make it home. Four of those have one credit and two have two credits. Day 1 closes at eight blobs after two births. Its net change is −2, one plant is left, average living age is 0.75 days, and average age at death is 0.00 because each death was of a founder on day 1. Day 2 opens with those eight blobs and a fresh food layout.

### 6.2 Quick Sim and generation extension

1. **Open.** Quick Sim shows the active run, displayed day, computational day or next dawn, last completed and saved days, and maximum. It defaults to **Additional generations** and offers **Complete through day**. In Watch live, pause the live tick while editing. If automatic preparation is active, take a safe-tick snapshot of its frontier and pause that job while choosing the target. Close without applying to restore the previous schedule. Opening the status of an already submitted Quick Sim leaves it running.
2. **Preview the destination.** Finish an uncommitted current day first, including a day at dawn, then add the entered count. When holding a completed day with no current day in progress, count from that completed boundary. Default to the smaller of 25 additional days and the available remaining count. Zero is valid when only the current day needs finishing. An absolute target includes that day. If it is complete, offer **Watch Day N**. If it exceeds the horizon, offer **More Generations**.
3. **Advance.** On **Simulate**, use the single authoritative writer to finish from the actual worker tick. Use the same actions, food, births, and deaths, and leave rendering out of the batch. Hold the visible pose. Show **Completed X of Y**, the current day, **Saved through Day N**, and measured pace once available. Commit only settled days to the ribbon and Trends. In Prepare and watch, an explicit target replaces the automatic job’s target. The playback day stays put.
4. **Stop or finish.** **Stop after this day** completes and saves the in-flight day. Disable repeated stop requests until the boundary. In Watch live, open the next unstarted dawn in Ready, or hold the final day. In a prepared run, stay on the displayed day and offer **Watch last day**, **Resume preparing to Day N**, and **Watch live from next dawn** where available.
5. **Keep working while browsing.** Close progress to keep the job visible in the header. Open recorded days or Trends while it works. Quick Sim from a replay acts on the active run’s frontier, with a preview that names both days. Keep the user’s replay or chart focused while publishing the new days. Changing active runs checkpoints or stops the previous writer first. After reload, restore the saved state and offer to continue to the previous target.

**Worked example.** Watch live is at Day 12, 4.2 seconds in. Enter 20 additional days. Quick Sim finishes the rest of Day 12 and completes Days 13–32. The archive ends at Day 32. If the horizon allows, World opens Day 33 at dawn. Starting at the settled end of Day 12 and entering 20 also ends at Day 32. An absolute target of 32 has the same result. If you were watching a recorded Day 3 while computation was partway through Day 12, the same request still targets Day 32 and leaves Day 3 on screen.

**More Generations** opens from the ribbon, its final tile, or Trends. Show the current limit and committed day. Accept a positive integer up to the number remaining before Day 1,000. Confirm the new limit and show the appended segment in the day strip as pending. In Prepare and watch, resume preparing. In Watch live, keep the current cursor and offer **Keep watching** or **Quick Sim**. Continue the existing food schedule beyond its final anchor using its last count. A same-seed rerun creates a fresh independent run. The dice opens a draft with a different valid seed.

## 7. Generation statistics and Trends

### 7.1 Counting contract

| Reading | Sample and interpretation |
| --- | --- |
| **Population** | Count living blobs at closing, including newborns. Show the opening count separately in the tooltip and export. Live shows blobs alive right now. |
| **Average age** | Mean completed days of the closing living population. Newborns contribute age 0. In Live, average the current ages of the blobs alive at the current tick. |
| **Average age at death** | Mean completed days at death for all blobs that died during that day, across all causes. In Live, include deaths recorded so far. A founder dying on Day 1 contributes a valid 0. |
| **Average speed** | Mean inherited speed trait of the closing living blobs. The inspector separately shows actual travel rate in world units/s. |
| **Average size** | Mean raw size trait of the closing living blobs. Body radius is a separate inspector value. |
| **Average sense range** | Mean inherited sense trait of the closing living blobs, in logical units. |
| **Births, deaths, and net** | Births assigned to the newborn's species. Deaths assigned to the victim's species and one of four causes. Net is births minus deaths and equals closing minus opening, within each scope. |
| **Food available and eaten** | Available is the full board's dawn plant count. Eaten is the plant count credited to the selected scope. Board leftovers equal available minus plant pieces eaten across all species. |
| **Creatures eaten** | Successful hunts credited to the hunter's scope. **Lost to predation** is an optional victim-side view. Globally both totals equal predation deaths. In a species view these two numbers can differ. |

Each average has sample count, mean, minimum, maximum, P25, P75, and both population and sample standard deviations. Compute percentiles by sorting the samples and interpolating linearly at index **p × (n − 1)**, using zero-based indices. Compute the squared deviations around the mean in a numerically stable pass. Divide by **n** for population variance and **n − 1** for sample variance. For ages 0, 2, 6, and 10, the mean is 4.5, P25 is 1.5, P75 is 7, and the standard deviations are approximately 3.84 and 4.43 respectively. A single value fills all five default lines with the same number and displays both deviations as zero. With no samples, store missing statistics and show dashes or chart gaps. The charts show literal zeros when a count is zero. All-species distributions pool the individuals. For example, species with two and twenty blobs contribute two and twenty age samples respectively. [Launch-era deviation calculation](https://github.com/minutelabsio/evolution-simulator/blob/3b99f358b70a608f5fb3e34fe8f410ba85aec7f3/src/wasm/src/math/stats.rs)

Use one daily row for **All species** and one for every species in the run, including extinct ones. Each row also records duration, opening, survival, closing, home arrivals, death causes, food supply, plants and prey eaten, victim-side losses, and five distributions. A versioned cohort label specifies **Closing living population** or **Deaths during day** beside the affected charts. Both spread styles use those cohorts. The launch-era source gathered the traits of creatures that took part in the day, including blobs that died. Keep opening counts and the World replay’s opening creature set so comparisons can explain that difference. In the guided chart-to-board lesson, also offer **Highlight chart cohort**: identify the day's closing survivors and use the closing view to include age-zero newborns, so the visitor can see which individuals contribute to the displayed spread. [Launch-era generation aggregates](https://github.com/minutelabsio/evolution-simulator/blob/3b99f358b70a608f5fb3e34fe8f410ba85aec7f3/src/wasm/src/api/mod.rs)

### 9.3 Reproducible and scalable execution

1. **Stable random streams.** Use a pinned deterministic generator and normal sampler. Derive streams from the user seed and stable numeric keys for day, spawn order, plant index, wander, and mutation. Tie choices are seeded and stable. Keep stream versions and any mutable counters in checkpoints. A seed, full setup and intervention sequence, and rule version identify a reproducible run. Test this across supported browsers. Camera, viewport, chart filters, and playback never feed the streams.
2. **Ordered model work.** Query sensing and threatened targets via a planar spatial grid. Use swept bounds for collision candidates, then compute actual contact times from the paths. Sort simultaneous events with the declared precedence. Within a tick, any changed energy, food availability, or target state is checked before later events are applied. Advance active blobs only. For a large sensing radius, query the occupied portion of the board rather than allocating cells outside it.
3. **Separate graphics from rules.** Grow or recycle instance buffers as needed. At wide zoom or high population, simplify meshes, batch eye and shadow draws, and keep detailed overlays for selected and nearby blobs. Show **Showing selected indicators** if the **All** layer is narrowed by the graphics budget. Preserve the exact count, selection list, and simulation outcomes. At low render frame rates, retain all events in data and combine short visual effects.
4. **Bound daily work by state and time.** Headless execution discards idle render frames and jumps to dusk once all blobs are home or dead. Extinct days still get the scheduled food count and zero population records. Report progress for long jobs and preserve any committed results when stopped. Changing size or population does not trigger a biological stop. The rule set itself constrains growth. Every closing blob requires a credit earned that day by a surviving parent. Thus closing population is at most plants eaten plus successful hunts. Large founding populations are valid even if many die immediately.
5. **Replay on demand.** Save a complete boundary checkpoint before Day 1, at least every 10 completed days, at branches, and after the most recent saved day. To play a day, start from the latest checkpoint before it begins and advance intervening days headlessly. Collect keyframes and events for the requested day. Cache the latest three tracks under a byte budget and evict the oldest under pressure. Use the same rebuild for Trait Space and dawn cohort views. Keep the published first-day track in memory while it is selected, even if the preparation frontier is far ahead.

### 9.4 Save, restore, and failure behavior

- During Live, save a resumable partial checkpoint when pausing, leaving the field, or after five wall-clock seconds of unsaved play. Keep it beside the latest completed boundary. For Live, preparation, and Quick Sim, save each completed day in one transaction. Write its summary rows, durable resume state, and new latest-day pointer, then clear its obsolete partial checkpoint in the same transaction. Keep periodic, branched, and initial checkpoints. Report a saved day after the transaction succeeds.
- Use one fenced writer per run. A second tab can read snapshots or create a copy. If the writer disappears, the next tab may resume after verifying ownership and reloading the saved pointer. Reject any older session's late commit. For a worker crash, retry from the saved checkpoint and show **Restored through Day N**. Preserve the day and partial tick if that exact checkpoint was saved.
- When storage becomes unavailable, keep the visible state and records in memory, distinguish **Completed** from **Saved**, and offer CSV and setup export. After space is available, backfill unsaved days in order and update the saved pointer only as each transaction succeeds. On library deletion, retain shared ancestor material while another run references it.
- Version validation is separate for app storage and biology. Migrate the storage schema where supported. Reconstruct an old run with its pinned rule version when available. If that engine version is absent, keep its saved Trends and CSV available and explain why continuation and reconstruction are unavailable.
- Validate imports before opening a run. Verify field ranges, unique species identities, ordering and extent of schedule points, and known rule versions. Preview the setup and interventions. A setup import recreates the experiment by advancing the engine. A raw CSV import belongs in an external analysis tool and has no simulation state.
