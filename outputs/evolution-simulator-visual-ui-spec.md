# Evolution Simulator
## Visual design and UI specification

The first frame is a white square tabletop suspended in a charcoal space. A thin gray lip and a visible sidewall give it thickness. Olive pellets dot the surface. Small steel-blue blobs wait around all four sides. Above the board, the navigation is light text and a single muted teal Settings pill. Below it, a thin teal time rail separates the stage from a near-black strip of tiny colored flowers. Selecting a blob brings the camera down behind its head. Its small black eyes, soft shadow, and dark scalloped thought bubble become legible at that distance. The camera, lights, and models create the depth. Every blob and pellet still has a position on the same flat simulation plane. [Opening World and controls](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6nMo8T3T0L4&t=60s), [Follow and sensing](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6nMo8T3T0L4&t=111s)

This is the visual companion to the [website and simulation plan](evolution-simulator-website-plan.md). **Reference** identifies a visible feature from the walkthrough. **Source** identifies a value from the dated April 22 launch snapshot or a calculation from that snapshot. **Site** identifies a concrete design choice for this project. Measurements in CSS pixels describe the proposed site at 100% browser zoom. A source material color is an input to lighting. Its appearance in a video frame varies with lighting, camera angle, and compression. [April 22 launch source](https://github.com/minutelabsio/evolution-simulator/tree/3b99f358b70a608f5fb3e34fe8f410ba85aec7f3)

The [implemented visual and interaction guide](evolution-simulator-implemented-ui.md) records the current site’s colors, dimensions, controls, and interaction states.

It uses illustrative values. ## 3. World layout and camera

### 3.1 Exact composition

At 1,920 × 1,080 the same dock leaves an 814 px stage, close to the filmed three-quarter-height stage. ### 3.2 Stage construction and camera presets

- **Board.** Use a 500 × 500 plane at Y = −0.05 with a 540 × 540 × 10 gray slab centered at Y = −6. The 20-unit exposed rim keeps the four borders easy to see at oblique angles. Use crisp corners with a restrained visual bevel of at most 1 unit. Keep the top clean white with a little shade only around contact. The rest of the stage is a flat charcoal void. Keep default homes implicit in the edge lineup. Show the dashed inset band when Homes is enabled. [Reference board geometry](https://github.com/minutelabsio/evolution-simulator/blob/3b99f358b70a608f5fb3e34fe8f410ba85aec7f3/src/components/generation-viewer.vue)
- **Lighting.** Start at 0.9 ambient fill and 0.25 directional key from [100, 200, −10], plus source fog starting at 1,000 and ending at 3,000 units. Use diffuse matte shading, antialiased silhouettes, and soft ground shadows. The near rim and its vertical wall must separate at opening zoom. In the cinematic close-up, a faint cast shadow should extend to the right in the reference camera orientation. Its direction stays consistent with the fixed key light during orbit. In Efficient quality use a soft projected contact ellipse per visible blob. [Reference scene and lights](https://github.com/minutelabsio/evolution-simulator/blob/3b99f358b70a608f5fb3e34fe8f410ba85aec7f3/src/components/generation-viewer.vue)
- **Board camera.** Start at [600, 300, 600] relative to board center with a 45° vertical field of view, near 1 and far 4,500. That is about 45° azimuth and 19.5° elevation. Orbit horizontally with damping, allow upward tilt to see all four edges, and stop above the tabletop. Wheel and pinch zoom around the board. **Fit board** returns the all-edges view. Keyboard controls in the camera menu offer the same orbit and zoom in discrete steps. [Launch camera and input](https://github.com/minutelabsio/evolution-simulator/blob/3b99f358b70a608f5fb3e34fe8f410ba85aec7f3/src/components/generation-viewer.vue)
- **Follow.** Use a camera goal 100 units behind the heading and 50 above the creature, looking about 30 above its origin, then adjust distance to keep the selected model and nearby floor visible. A typical starter blob is 35–58 px tall in the focus shot. Ease at the feel of 5% goal interpolation per frame at 60 Hz, roughly 1 second to settle. Restore the saved Board orbit and zoom in about 1.5 seconds. Keep the head below the top chrome. Preserve heading when the blob is still. If the target dies, hold its final pose briefly, keep the record in Details, and return to the board. [Filmed low camera](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6nMo8T3T0L4&t=113s), [source Follow offsets and smoothing](https://github.com/minutelabsio/evolution-simulator/blob/3b99f358b70a608f5fb3e34fe8f410ba85aec7f3/src/components/generation-viewer.vue)
- **Overhead.** Look straight down with a fixed north-up view and even margins. Fit the outer slab into 76% of the shorter available stage dimension. Use a projection where a floor circle looks circular and the four 500-unit sides measure the same. The Overhead chip gets a checkmark. Switching back restores the last oblique view. Use this preset for same-scale comparisons of sense circles and food distributions. [Overhead in the walkthrough](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6nMo8T3T0L4&t=348s)

## 4. Blobs, food, overlays, and motion
